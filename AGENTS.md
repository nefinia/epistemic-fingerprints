# Writing and editorial instructions

Apply these instructions to every written artifact in this repository, including the paper, pilot report, website, README, notebook prose, funding applications, project descriptions, and messages drafted from this project.

## Register by artifact

- Research paper and protocol: precise, intellectually confident, cautious about claims, and human rather than impersonal. Questions may motivate a section, but evidence and definitions must carry the argument.
- Website and project descriptions: curious, interdisciplinary, accessible, and grounded in the actual experiment. Connect technical questions to why they matter for people and collective knowledge.
- README and notebook: direct, practical, and welcoming. Explain what the reader can run, see, or contribute.
- Discord and collaborator messages: warm, spontaneous, concise, and genuinely open to exchange. Do not polish away Sofia's natural questioning voice.
- Funding and fellowship applications: ambitious but concrete. Lead from the research question, pilot evidence, proposed work, and expected outputs; avoid startup language and inflated impact claims.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- Preserve the distinction between behavioral differentiation and evidence of consciousness or moral status.
- Never invent results, credentials, collaborators, commitments, citations, dates, or funding arrangements.
- Label simulated data clearly and never describe it as empirical evidence.
- Prefer concrete examples, methods, observations, and next steps over abstract promotional claims.
- Keep Sofia visible as an astrophysicist, interdisciplinary researcher, artist, and project-builder when personal context is relevant; do not force the biography into technical passages where it is not relevant.
- Avoid generic AI prose, corporate filler, repeated rhetorical triads, excessive headings, overuse of em dashes, and formulaic constructions such as "not only X, but Y."
- Do not make every paragraph end in a slogan or polished conclusion. Leave genuine research questions open when they are open.
- Retain slight natural irregularity and personal rhythm in informal writing. Do not deliberately add grammatical errors.

## Editorial check

Before considering a written artifact complete, ask:

1. Does it sound like a curious scientist and artist thinking through a real problem?
2. Is the central question clear without overselling the answer?
3. Are claims matched to evidence and uncertainty?
4. Is the invitation to contribute or the next step concrete?
5. Could generic AI-generated phrasing be replaced with a more direct sentence?
