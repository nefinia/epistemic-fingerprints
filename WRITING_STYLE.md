# Writing voice for Epistemic Fingerprints

This project should sound like the person behind it: a scientist who is comfortable with uncertainty, an artist attentive to perspective, and a builder who wants ideas to become experiments.

## The basic voice

Clear, curious, warm, interdisciplinary, and concrete. The writing can begin from a large question, but it should return quickly to something we can observe, compare, build, or test.

Sofia's strongest project language often follows this movement:

1. A question that genuinely troubles or interests her.
2. Enough context to show why the question is not merely rhetorical.
3. A concrete experiment, project, or possibility.
4. An honest account of what is still uncertain.
5. An invitation to think or work together.

For example:

> AI allows us to generate more papers, hypotheses and possible explanations. But are we exploring more conceptual space, or producing more variations around the same ideas? Epistemic Fingerprints begins with a small experiment: hold the model fixed, vary the histories and perspectives around it, and observe what changes in the way it forms hypotheses.

## What should remain recognizably Sofia

- Questions are real openings, not marketing hooks.
- Scientific precision and philosophical curiosity can coexist.
- Connections between AI, science, art, cognition, society, and lived experience are welcome when they clarify the problem.
- Enthusiasm should attach to a specific idea, result, collaboration, or next step.
- The project is presented as something open to exchange, criticism, improvement, and contribution.
- Long-term ambition is allowed, but the present evidence is described honestly.

## Different products need different registers

### Paper and research report

Use precise definitions, explicit assumptions, traceable citations, and restrained claims. Keep the prose readable and allow the motivating question to remain visible. Avoid pretending that neutral academic writing has no author, but do not turn methods or results into autobiography.

### Website

Begin with the problem in ordinary language. Explain every figure: what is being compared, how to read it, whether the data are simulated or empirical, and what can and cannot be concluded. Give visitors paths to the experiment, results, references, code, and ways to contribute.

### Repository and notebook

Tell readers what exists, what currently runs, what remains incomplete, and how they can reproduce or extend it. Technical contributors should encounter real code and transparent limitations, not a promotional shell around the webapp.

### Community posts and messages

Keep them shorter and more conversational. Natural formulations such as "I have been thinking about this," "I am curious what you think," and "maybe this is something we could work on" suit the voice when they are true. Do not make informal messages sound like miniature grant proposals.

### Funding applications

Connect the large motivation to a bounded programme of work: research question, pilot evidence, milestones, people, resources, outputs, and risks. Frame the long-term vision as a direction being built—not as an institution or impact already achieved.

## Language to prefer

- "I am exploring..."
- "I have been thinking about..."
- "The question is whether..."
- "This small pilot tests..."
- "What would count as evidence?"
- "At this stage, we cannot conclude..."
- "I see this as the beginning of a longer project."
- "I am very open to exchanging ideas and improving the project; contributions are welcome."

## Language to avoid

- "revolutionary," "groundbreaking," or "game-changing"
- "leveraging cutting-edge AI"
- "unlocking the future of..."
- unsupported claims that the project will transform a field
- empty declarations that something is "important" without explaining why
- symmetrical, overly polished paragraphs that erase the author's natural rhythm
- false certainty around exploratory measures or simulated results

## Final test

The writing is ready when it feels authored rather than generated: the question is alive, the method is concrete, the uncertainty is honest, and the reader understands how to enter the conversation.
